# Lyrics [trials 88–93, 96–103, 109–121] — about 30 trials, four designs before the one that stayed
**Go straight to:** word onsets (page `timing`) → design sheet per section over the real composited frames → owner approves → per word choose frosted glass or negative window by contrast → render one see-through clip on its own top track.

**What the owner rejected and why**
1. Gradient words, centred, static, caption-timed → "the texts aren't static… glassy/transparent". [90]
2. The first type study was read off 400 px tiles and missed that the words are glass and grow on the beat. Study type on full-resolution crops; say what a build leaves out of the study. [90, 91]

**What works**
- Glass words anchored on him, growing with a step on the syllable; ALL words, not only main words. [92, 93, 96]
- "Placing matters for the art of it": no word centred by default; placement comes from the shot's depth, its lines, the action, and the edit's effects (masthead behind his head, words on the road, on a tower, inside bands, in a comic panel). Design sheet first. [99–103]
- Negative windows (letters show the picture as a negative): strong on suit, sky and dark pictures; dies on busy mid-tones (a word vanished) → check each word's contrast on its real frame. [109–114]
- Hook: frosted glass + glitch — the letters are the picture itself, blurred 14 px and lifted, white ring, 12 px colour split; on each syllable the glass breaks into a hard split with jumping bands. Owner: "Perfect. This is good. I love the glitch." Works because it borrows colour from the shot, reacts on the syllable, and never inverts him. [115–118]

**Gotchas:** CSS masks loaded from another path are hidden by Chrome (use a data URI) [101]; an anchor fitted from 3 frames overshot when the move slowed — measure every frame [111]; re-make pages after a font change [119]; one font for every title.
