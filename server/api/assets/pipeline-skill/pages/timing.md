# Timing and the cut [log §1, §3; trials 4, 5, 8, 11, 14, 15, 26, 89, 92, 97]
**Song map first.** `listen` in windows (0.1 s levels + bass hits) and `song_notes`: write a table of drop, bursts, silences, bars, and the hits inside a bar. Every later decision (freeze points, number of panels, where a word lands) comes from that table. The owner approved draft 1's timing on first play ("on point").

**Shot rules**
- Every shot starts on a hit; its length is the gap to the next hit.
- Quiet intro (no bass) → long shots, slowed slightly so actions land on soft hits. Bursts (7 hits in ~2 s) → 0.2–0.4 s action shots, one per hit. Silences after a burst → one held pose (future freeze / cut-out moments). Full beat (4 s bars) → e.g. 1.6 s action, 0.3 s flash shot, 1.0 s, 1.1 s.
- Time the START of an action to the hit, not its middle (he was already mid-air on the hit). [5]
- Contact = a lead-in shot that ENDS on the hit + the contact frame as the FIRST frame after it. [14]
- Length follows the song and the last frame, not the reference or a round number. Draft 1 copied the reference length and ended before the song started. [8]
- "It needs to end strong" took three endings: plan the ending on the song map. [11, 15]
- A 3-frame flash on a 0.2 s shot ate it → merge the short shot into its neighbour; lengthen only by taking time from a neighbour so later cuts stay on beat. [26]
- When the owner says a beat is "barely seen", give the scene more notes; do not shave the effect.
- Lay a whole section with one patch (50 shots at once), then check the middle frame of EVERY shot: a 0.3 s shot taken 0.5 s off is a different picture. [4]

**Word timing.** Caption times were up to 150 ms off. Measure word onsets from a voice-only curve: cancel the beat (subtract the song shifted by one loop length) and read onsets in 10 ms steps. `auto_captions` is only for rough times, then undo it (it always draws captions). [89, 92, 97]
